---
title: Getting Experience
summary: How to build three portfolio pieces with real results, write them up the way recruiters expect, and avoid side-job scams.
---
Certificates get you an interview; real results get you the job. Aim to have three portfolio pieces by month 5. Each one should show the goal, what you did, the numbers, and what you would change next time.

## Build experience in three steps

1. **Practice pieces (months 2–3).** Before you have clients, make samples for an imaginary business or a friend's shop. For example: nine Instagram posts for a local café, twenty recruiting posts for X, or a one-month content calendar for an online shop ([@SOHO guide](https://atsoho.com/blog/sns-agency-side-job-guide)).
2. **One real small campaign (months 3–4).** Offer to run ads for a friend's shop, a relative's business or a local nonprofit, with a small budget of ¥10,000–30,000. Even modest results, reported honestly with a screenshot from GA4 or the ad platform, beat any certificate.
3. **Paid side work (month 4 onward).** クラウドワークス, ランサーズ and ココナラ list many social media (SNS運用) and ad-operations jobs. Social media management is the easiest place to start. Pay rises when you add strategy and analysis, not just posting ([AI Picks on agency rates](https://aipicks.jp/mag/ai-sns-agency-pricing-rates-2026)).

## How to write up each piece

Japanese recruiters look for the same five things in every project ([Remedy](https://remedy-tokyo.co.jp/media/10948/)). Write each piece in this order:

1. **施策 (what you did):** the campaign, posts or page, and its goal.
2. **仮説 (your hypothesis):** why you thought it would work.
3. **改善 (what you changed):** what you adjusted after the first results.
4. **結果 (the result):** the numbers, such as clicks, followers, purchases or cost per customer.
5. **再現性 (why it would work again):** what you learned that you could repeat for another client.

Keep each piece to one or two pages. A full portfolio is usually 5–10 pages ([Recruit Agent](https://www.r-agent.com/guide/start/12572/)).

## Avoid side-job scams

Fake jobs aimed at beginners are common on these platforms. Watch for:

- Clients with no identity verification.
- Requests to move the conversation off the platform before any work starts ([CrowdWorks forum](https://crowdworks.jp/consultation/threads/18550)).
- Any job that asks you to pay for training, a “starter kit” or materials. This is always a scam.

## Client permission

Never post a real client's numbers in your portfolio without permission. Many contracts forbid it. If you can't get permission, show percentages or changes instead of the actual figures, and leave out the client's name.
